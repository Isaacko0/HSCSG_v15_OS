# / INNOVATION DOMAINS

1. **Collective Intelligence** — The emergent capacity of groups to think, learn, and act together more effectively than any individual alone. Collective intelligence arises through well-designed feedback loops, shared sense-making, and participatory governance practices.
2. **Capital Allocation** — Redesigning how value flows through networks to align capital with care. This includes cooperative finance, public-interest investment, and regenerative funding mechanisms that prioritize long-term resilience over short-term extraction.
3. **Knowledge & Data Commoning** — The collective stewardship of information, research, and cultural assets as shared resources. Knowledge commoning transforms intellectual property into intellectual ecology — enabling continuous learning, adaptation, and contribution.
4. **Digital Public Infrastructure** — Foundational digital systems — identity, data, payments, communications — built as commons rather than platforms. DPI ensures equitable access, interoperability, and democratic governance of the tools societies depend on.
5. **Open Protocols** — Shared technical and social standards that allow diverse systems to interoperate without permission or control. Open protocols create the connective tissue for networks of networks — enabling trust, composability, and innovation across communities.
6. **Bioregional Coordination** — Place-based governance that organizes collaboration around the natural boundaries of ecosystems rather than political borders. It aligns human activity with ecological realities, fostering stewardship, resilience, and shared responsibility within a living landscape.
7. **Network Governance** — A decentralized model of coordination where autonomous actors — individuals, organizations, or communities — collaborate through shared principles, trust, and mutual accountability rather than centralized authority.
8. **Rights of Nature** — A legal and ethical framework recognizing ecosystems as rights-bearing entities with intrinsic value. The Rights of Nature movement redefines justice to include the living Earth as a subject of care and stewardship — ensuring that governance systems protect and restore ecological integrity.
9. **Regenerative Finance & Economy** — Economic models that restore and sustain the health of ecological and social systems. Regenerative finance aligns incentives with renewal rather than depletion, redistributing value to where it enhances community wellbeing, biodiversity, and long-term planetary balance.
10. **Impact Evaluation** — Measuring and learning from the outcomes of social, ecological, and economic interventions through participatory and systemic lenses. Impact evaluation shifts from narrow metrics of efficiency or growth toward understanding contribution, vitality, and resilience within living systems.
11. **Digital Democracy** — Technological systems that expand participatory governance and civic agency in the digital era. Digital democracy includes tools for deliberation, collective decision-making, and transparent accountability, enabling more direct, distributed, and responsive forms of public power.
12. **Currency Design** — The intentional creation of exchange systems that reflect shared values and reinforce healthy social and ecological relationships. Currency design explores complementary, mutual-credit, and regenerative forms of value circulation that strengthen communities and commons rather than extract from them.
13. **Citizen Science** — Collaborative research processes where community members generate, interpret, and act upon data relevant to their own environments. Citizen science democratizes knowledge production, fostering local stewardship, transparency, and accountability in scientific inquiry.
14. **Cosmolocal Networks** — Frameworks that connect human systems to deeper cosmological patterns — cycles of time, energy, and interbeing. Cosmological networks integrate indigenous knowledge, spiritual insight, and scientific understanding to align governance and technology with living planetary rhythms.
15. **Public Dialogue & Facilitation** — Practices that enable open, inclusive, and constructive conversation across differences. Public dialogue cultivates mutual understanding, shared purpose, and collective capacity for action. Facilitation grounds this process in care, equity, and adaptive process design — ensuring every voice can contribute to shaping the commons.
16. **Metacrisis Awareness** — Cultivating the capacity to perceive interconnected crises — ecological, social, technological, and spiritual — as symptoms of a deeper systemic imbalance. Metacrisis awareness invites transformation at the level of worldview, not just policy or technology.
17. **Mutual Aid Networks** — Horizontal community networks where members directly support each other's survival and wellbeing through voluntary reciprocity rather than charity or state provision. Mutual aid fosters collective decision-making, shared resources, and solidarity economics.
18. **Local Food Networks** — Decentralized community-based food systems that prioritize relationships, sustainability, and food sovereignty over industrial agriculture. Local food networks build regional economic resilience while connecting producers and consumers through shared governance and mutual benefit.
19. **Public Interest AI** — The development, deployment, and governance of artificial intelligence systems in service of public good rather than narrow commercial interests. Public interest AI advances human rights, democratic values, and community participation in how AI shapes society.
